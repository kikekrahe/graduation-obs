![[Schmieg.png]]
I came across the following image in the book [[A Cat, a Dog, a Microwave]]. It is part of the process derived from Sebastian Schmieg's project [This is the Problem, the Solution, the Past and the Future](https://sebastianschmieg.com/this-is-the-problem-the-solution-the-past-and-the-future/) a dataset which was trained on humans to categorise certain images to the four categories of past, future, problem and solution. This methodology inspires me, possibly acting as a form of participatory research which I can utilise within my research.

Thinking of [[Rubinstein, Daniel - 1. Introduction to the  Philosophy of the Digital Image]], this operation and experiment is an exercise in the politics of representation. 
If [[representation]] is the role which is taken by images when dealing with digital machines, then interpretation is integral. This interpretation happens through the intrinsically human process of resemblance, where a certain judgement is necessary in order to make decisions regarding the categorisation of images. This process in and of itself is a political role, given the fact that a certain role of power and authority is implied and required from this 'judge'. This interpretation lies at the heart of every AI Image Database in the mere pairing of image-key carried out by 'ghost-workers' and [[mechanical turk]].
