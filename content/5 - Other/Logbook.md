![[Schmieg.png]]
I came across the following image in the book [[A Cat, a Dog, a Microwave]]. It is part of the process derived from Sebastian Schmieg's project [This is the Problem, the Solution, the Past and the Future](https://sebastianschmieg.com/this-is-the-problem-the-solution-the-past-and-the-future/) a dataset which was trained on humans to categorise certain images to the four categories of past, future, problem and solution. This methodology inspires me, possibly acting as a form of participatory research which I can utilise within my research.

Thinking of [[Rubinstein, Daniel - 1. Introduction to the  Philosophy of the Digital Image]], this operation and experiment is an exercise in the politics of representation. 
If [[representation]] is the role which is taken by images when dealing with digital machines, then interpretation is integral. This interpretation happens through the intrinsically human process of resemblance, where a certain judgement is necessary in order to make decisions regarding the categorisation of images. This process in and of itself is a political role, given the fact that a certain role of power and authority is implied and required from this 'judge'. This interpretation lies at the heart of every AI Image Database in the mere pairing of image-key carried out by 'ghost-workers' and [[mechanical turk]].

# [16.03.25]

The traditional image and its genealogy is ordered in an arboreal structure, every product is able to point back a certain point of origin. 
The digital image, even with this in mind, also has another form and shape of existence, one which is rhizomatic in structure and alludes to the absence of a point of origin but rather relies on multiplicities. This is fully the case in the artificial generation of images. 

[![Organisation Development and Rhizomatic thinking](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/C5112AQEMSnCZR__rSw/article-inline_image-shrink_1000_1488/article-inline_image-shrink_1000_1488/0/1526288349750?e=1745452800&v=beta&t=zbRG6CCeyERe64CoElHiCjnA8E36rGqiOxxhGW6psjM)

The digital image is not only the visual it carries with it but more importantly it mode of transportation and distribution and digital proliferation which led it to there. It's only visual but also performatic; visible and invisible at the same time. 

The poor image carries its transportation and journey with it as a sort of scar, a visual representation of events and happenings. 

After some research I came across the term [Adversarial Noise], a term used primarily in image politics and protests as a form of confusing AI systems into 'confusing' images for what they are not. This could be an interesting point of research if possible to combine with poor images.
