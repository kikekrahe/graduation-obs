A.
At the time of the beginning of this project, I’m interested in exploring the topic of collective forgetting in periods of technological acceleration. In particular, as it relates to transitions of media and information. 
I came upon this idea when reading about the pact of forgetting carried out in Spain in 1975, something which awakened in me this interest in the opposite of the thematic in many of my projects: forgetting instead of remembering. 
Collective forgetting in periods of technological acceleration refers to the widening of the information and language gap, the narrative fluency, between modes of information dissemination considered as ‘past’ (think archives, libraries, paintings, books etc) and ‘contemporary’ (short-form visual content, TikTok, memes, reels etc).
The drive behind the research is to analyse what impact the ‘transformation’ and ‘deformation’ of content has on the growing catalogue of past media; and more importantly, to attempt to reflect on this through the lens of forgetting. 
Federico Campagna’s research on world-ing and his definition of reality as a variable within each era of the world and civilisation seems to serve as a potentially strong resource regarding the ubiquitous sense of our current timeline, as we know it, coming to an end. 
Consequently, I aim to position my research within the (potential) intersection between these eras of the world and civilisation associated with ‘realities’.
So I ask:
— Does all information that is deteriorating in its constant idleness and lack of evolution call for a remoulding?
— If so, what does this remoulding look like?
— Or would it be more productive to institute a sort of collective forgetting?

B.
My research question is:
How does the acceleration of media and information transitions impact the processes of collective forgetting, and to what extend can this act serve as a response to the deformation of historical memory?

C.
Throughout the process of my research I want to iterate and explore through various methods of creation, preferably honing on those that would allow me to expand my current toolkit. Without a certain goal (and form) in mind for the practice project, I am hopeful about exploring and working with methods such as audiovisual creation, synthetic image creation and fashion design. 
Tied to these is my methodology, for which, with the same uncertainty as the method, I see myself utilising a large amount of context research (in particular, the exploration of transitions of media and information throughout history, and civilisation’s response). Additionally, I find that participatory research could play a large role as well, allowing me to ground the topic in actual testimonies and adapt it with the responses of participants. 
I feel that partners and collaborators would be valuable for my work, however I’m yet uncertain whether and how to incorporate these. Institutions of archival and archaeological institutes feel like relevant bodies of knowledge for this investigation; this is something I will inquire into further during the first phase of my project.
