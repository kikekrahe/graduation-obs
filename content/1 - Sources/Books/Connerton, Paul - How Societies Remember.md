# Connerton, Paul - How Societies Remember

[2]

> We experience our present world in a context which is casually connected with past events and objects, and hence with reference to events and objects which we are not experiencing when we are experiencing the present.

Our present reality is affected by the past to which events and objects are connected. In a different present scenario, we are affected by different past connections. Our present condition, then, influences the way we perceive and ingest these past connections; but our past connections, more importantly, affect our present condition.

[3]

> Concerning social memory in particular, we may note that images of the past commonly legitimate a present social order.

Past images serve as an indication of social memory concerning a present social order and are used to justify it. Although sometimes not lived, these images instil a sense of past happening. 

> Across generations, different sets of memories, frequently in the shape of implicit background narratives, will encounter each other; so that, although physically present to one another in a particular setting, the different generations may remain mentally and emotionally insulated, the memories of one generation locked irretrievably, as it were, in the brains and bodies of that generation.

In a way, the subjects present to a generation and a 'reality' act as a medium and embodiment for its information as well. Although more selective than some physical media, their embodiment is more total and all-encompassing. 

[[> example]]
> Their conversation was unintelligible because the two of them had lived in the same social world but with an interval of twenty-five years. Thus although for ordinary speech she and Marcel used the same language, when it came to names - when it came, we might say, to their seeking to exchange a socially legitimate currency of  memories - their vocabularies had nothing in common. [[Proust, Marcel - Swann's Way]]

The conversation between Marcel and a young American woman can be seen as an example of how bodies with different background narratives, who lived different times, come to interact between each other.

[6]

> The world of the percipient, defined in terms of temporal experience, is an organised body of expectations based on recollection.

All beginnings start with recollection. Our conceptual framework is established based on previous experiences in order to be intelligible at all. We act within a system of expectations.
