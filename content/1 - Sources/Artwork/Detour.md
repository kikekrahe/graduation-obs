# Detour

[![pic 1](http://www.matthewbuckingham.net/images/detour1.jpg)](http://www.matthewbuckingham.net/images/detour1.jpg)
[![pic 2](http://www.matthewbuckingham.net/images/detour2.jpg)](http://www.matthewbuckingham.net/images/detour2.jpg)

With this installation, Buckingham makes historical information public in a certain context relevant to that information (that way also addressing the immediate audience). 
This **modern monument** addresses and informs about the founding of Los Angeles by  indigenous communities, and contrasting it to the present-day position of these. 
He addresses the social amnesia of Los Angeles. 

> Although access to the bench was unrestricted, many of the people who sat with their backs against Buckingham's poster might not have had fast access to the internet; indeed, one might ask, how many could even read the English text in the website? Fragmenting his work between cyberspace and the physical space of the street meant that this question necessarily became pressing. Thus, while he suggested a new form for a monument, Buckingham also articulated the conditions of any attempt to address a public, and showed that attempts to restore memory are often compromised by existing economic inequalities.

The contradictions between accessibility and inaccessibility, both physically, digitally and culturally present a challenge for the viewer, adding to the urgency behind the monument.
