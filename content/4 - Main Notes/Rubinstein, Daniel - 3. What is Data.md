An analog image is an image which has a direct connection to its negative, to a certain location (its point of origin) the same way that an apple, after being watered, is directly responsible for the product of the apple tree. Its existence is continuous, comfortable, conservative. Deleuze argues that representation imposes a static, hierarchical structure on the world, privileging identity over difference and sameness over multiplicity.

Another, more powerful (according to Deleuze), method is the post-modern one; the digital. The digital image, on the other hand, is data. It's malleable because its fundamental principles rely on binary and multi-input data which is available for modification by the user. Just through the manipulation of this data, one image can be completely transformed into another one.
In other words, the digital image, with its capacity for manipulation, simulation and infinite reproducibility, challenges the notion of the stable analog image. Instead of the representation of this pre-existing reality, it creates its own. This copy without an original, he terms as the [[simulacrum]].

In this post-modern digital age, the self is no longer a stable, unified entity. Instead, it's fragmented across different platforms and spaces, rather a **dividual** than an individual. This dividual is constantly reconfigured by the networks and infrastructures in which it participates.
